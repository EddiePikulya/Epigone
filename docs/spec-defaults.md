# Spec defaults (carried into /to-spec)

Working defaults agreed during the 2026-07-09 grilling session. Not architecture (see `docs/adr/`), not glossary (see `CONTEXT.md`) — tunable starting values for the spec.

- **Stream poll interval: 10 seconds** (2026-07-09 set 30s; 20s then 10s on 2026-07-29, to cut the signal latency the A4 copy executor inherits). Each wallet in the poll set costs weight 4 per poll — one clearinghouseState call per POSITION_VENUE (core + xyz, see the covered-venues bullet) — i.e. **24 weight/min/wallet** at 10s (12 at 20s, 8 at 30s). The poll set is tracked wallets UNION Users' own linked wallets (issue #121), so a linked wallet adds the same weight-4 as a tracked one.

  **Saturation point, not headroom.** The 900/min shared bucket is fully consumed by position polling alone at roughly **37 wallets at 10s** (75 at 20s, 112 at 30s). Add order polling at its 100s cadence (24 weight/min/wallet, next bullets) and the stream as a whole saturates at **~18 wallets** — that combined figure is the real ceiling. It is reachable by very few users: with a 5-wallet follow cap and a 15-wallet admin tier, the operator plus one tester is up to 20 addresses, already past it. This is explicitly accepted while Epigone runs for one operator plus a tester (decision 2026-07-29); it is *not* a safe default if the user count or per-user tracking grows. Saturation degrades silently rather than failing: the stream keeps priority and its 120-weight reserve, but ingest starves (fine metrics go stale) and passes run back-to-back, so the *effective* interval drifts above the configured one. The structural fix is moving the stream off the REST budget entirely (websocket lane — separate limits: 10 connections, 1000 subscriptions, no REST weight), not tuning this constant.

  **Do not measure a poll-interval change by total weight consumed.** The two consumers of the shared bucket behave differently. The stream is *inelastic*: its demand is exactly `wallets × 4 / interval`, and once a pass finishes it sleeps. Ingest is *elastic*: with ~6.6k fine-eligible traders on refresh cadences and 42k coarse rows, its backlog is effectively unbounded, so it consumes whatever the stream leaves. Consequently total spend sits near the 900/min pacing target whenever ingest has due work, largely independent of the poll interval — a poll-interval change does not raise total consumption, it **reallocates** the bucket from ingest to the stream (the stream has priority and a 120-weight reserve, so it never starves itself).

  Measure the cost as **ingest throughput** — fine refreshes per minute — not as total weight. On 2026-07-29 the 20s→10s change measured ~4 refreshes/min before and after: no detectable cost at a 6-wallet poll set, where the extra weight is a small fraction of the bucket and lost in ingest's own burstiness. The stable, decision-relevant figures are the saturation point above and the `rate_limit_events` count (0 all-time as of 2026-07-29, sampled spend 696–969/min at 10s before the venue drop — minute-level overshoot past 900 is expected when fills surcharges settle as debt, per `Budget.settle`).

  This entry previously carried a "~124 weight/min" baseline (single read of `rate_budget.minute_spent`, used to justify 30s→20s) and then a "~230–320/min steady state" correction. Both were sampling artifacts of ingest's backlog rather than properties of the poll interval; neither should be treated as a baseline, and re-measuring total weight to evaluate the next interval change will reproduce the same confusion.
- **Covered venues: core + xyz** (operator decision 2026-07-29). `mkts` (Markets by Kinetiq index perps — `mkts:US500`, `mkts:QQQ`) was dropped from `POSITION_VENUES`; the position and order pollers walk that one tuple in lockstep (the #31 rule), so it left both at once. Per wallet per pass that takes positions from weight 6 to **4** and orders from 60 to **40**. At the shipped 10s position interval the 900/min bucket saturates on position polling alone at ~37 wallets (900 ÷ 24), up from ~25 at weight 6; at 30s it would be ~112 (900 ÷ 8), up from ~75. Order polling adds 24 weight/min/wallet at its 100s cadence (next bullet), so the two together saturate at **~18 wallets** at the shipped 10s interval — the position-only figure is the headline, the combined one the real ceiling. "Saturate" here means the stream alone consuming the whole 900/min bucket, i.e. ingest starved to nothing; the stream reserve decides who loses first, so the practical ceiling sits below these numbers by whatever ingest throughput is worth keeping. `MKTS_DEX` and the mkts focus-market listings stay defined: re-enabling is putting the constant back in the tuple. Migration 0027 purges any surviving `mkts:` snapshot rows, because a coin that stops being fetched diffs identically to a coin that closed. (Focus-market classification is deliberately unchanged — it is fed by account-wide fills, not by what we poll, and already maps dexs Epigone has never polled.)
- **Order-poll cadence: 100 seconds** (`DEFAULT_ORDER_POLL_INTERVAL_SECONDS`, `epigone.config`; operator decision 2026-07-29, down from 300). Resting-order diffing (issue #115) costs `ORDERS_WEIGHT` × 2 covered venues = **40 weight per wallet per cycle** (20 nominal per call; ~8 measured, so ~16 real), i.e. **24 nominal weight/min/wallet** at 100s. With position polling's 24/min/wallet at the shipped 10s interval that is 48/min/wallet, so the stream alone claims the whole 900/min bucket at ~18 distinct wallets. Order spends carry the stream reserve, so a mis-tuned cadence costs order-alert latency, never Position Alerts. Structural limit on the pass itself: the pass spaces wallets by `ORDER_WALLET_SPACING_SECONDS` (5s) and each wallet's two sends hold the #41 send gate ~2s, so N wallets take ~7N−5 seconds — ~15 wallets exactly fills a 100s cycle and a bigger poll set runs passes back-to-back (the loop sleeps `max(0, interval − elapsed)`). That degrades latency, not budget: the same spacing ceilings order polling at ~5.7 weight/s (~343/min) regardless of wallet count.
- **Rate budget: one shared 900 weight/min bucket** (issue #28, 2026-07-10). Ingest + stream draw from a single Postgres-backed token bucket — 75% of Hyperliquid's 1200/min per-IP cap, i.e. 25% steady-state headroom; burst capped at 240 so even the worst rolling minute (full burst + a minute of refill = 1140) stays under the cap. The stream has priority: ingest never draws the bucket below a 120-weight reserve floor. The gateway backs off and retries on 429 (`Retry-After`, else exponential + jitter); a persistent streak surfaces as `RateLimitedError`, which passes treat as pacing — retry later, never abort.
- **Rate calibration** (issue #41, 2026-07-11). Two additions to the shared bucket eliminated the residual 429 trickle. (1) **Real fills weight**: per HL's rate-limit docs, `userFills` costs 20 *plus* weight per 20 fills returned (up to ~+100 on a full ~2000-fill response) — the root cause of 429s at a nominally under-budget ~600/min. The fine pass settles the surcharge (`ceil(fills/20)`, `FILLS_PER_SURCHARGE_WEIGHT`) against the budget post-response via `Budget.settle`, which may drive the bucket into debt that later spends pace off. (2) **Send gate** (`SMOOTHING_WEIGHT_PER_SECOND = 20`, i.e. the cap spread uniformly per second): every grant reserves a window proportional to its weight, so the bucket can't be emptied in a sub-second spike; the gate bounds a stream poll's worst wait to one ingest call's window plus its settled surcharge (≈6s for a full ~2000-fill response — most of a cycle at the current 10s poll interval, so a pass delayed behind ingest lands late rather than skipping), keeping the reserve-floor priority intact. Observability: the budget row meters real consumed weight and logs actual-vs-limit each minute (`epigone.budget`). Note: HL's `userRateLimit` info endpoint was probed (2026-07-11) and reports *address-based action* limits (`nRequestsUsed`/`nRequestsCap`), not IP info-request weight — it always reads 0 for us, hence the budget-side metering instead. Constants are starting values; final tuning is empirical against the production server's IP after merge.
- **Per-User follow cap: 5 wallets, 15 for the admin** (`MAX_TRACKED_WALLETS` / `ADMIN_MAX_TRACKED_WALLETS`, `epigone.bot.handlers`; operator decision 2026-07-29). The first guard on the poll set (issue #23): every tracked wallet costs poller weight on every pass, so an unbounded list lets one User exhaust the global ceiling. The owner (issue #33) previously followed without limit; they now carry the old global 15 as a real ceiling — a higher tier, not a waiver. The cap is enforced at the one shared `track_address` seam, so the screener, criteria-results and profile Follow taps all obey it, and the limit-reached toast quotes whichever number applies to that User.
- **Metrics refresh tiers:** full-universe scan is an ~11+ hour rate-limited batch (run rarely); active/tracked/screener-visible traders refresh incrementally (hourly–daily); dormant accounts swept weekly.
- **Two-stage scan (discovered via Bullpen R&D 2026-07-09):** HL's info API `portfolio` request returns precomputed windowed PnL / account value / volume (day, week, month, allTime, perp variants) for any address in a single weight-20 call — verified live via `bullpen hyperliquid stats --address`. Stage 1 (coarse): portfolio stats across the Universe → cheap windowed PnL/ROI/volume metrics, one call per account, no fill paging. Stage 2 (fine): fills-based metrics (win rate, avg win/loss, Sharpe, drawdown) only for accounts passing coarse filters (likely a few thousand). Screener filters should distinguish coarse metrics (available Universe-wide) from fine metrics (available for the vetted subset).
- **Fine-metrics backfill via S3 (ecosystem survey 2026-07-09):** Hyperliquid publishes raw fills to official S3 buckets (`hl-mainnet-node-data/node_fills_by_block`, `hyperliquid-archive`; free third-party mirror: Reservoir). Prefer bulk-downloading fills and computing fine metrics offline for the whole Universe over rate-limited API paging; the API budget then serves only incremental freshness + the stream. Verify bucket access/cost/lag at build time.
- **Alert noise controls** (issue #10): a **minimum position size** (`users.min_size_usd` global, `tracks.min_size_usd` per-Track override) suppresses alerts for positions notionally smaller than the floor, across every alert kind that is about a position — Withdrawal Alerts (#171) are exempt, because the floor judges a position's notional and a withdrawal is a fact about the account with no notional to judge; **mute** (`tracks.muted`) silences a Track without unfollowing, Withdrawal Alerts included. Both are edited from `/tracked`. Suppression happens at queue time in the stream poller, never at delivery — so unmuting or raising a floor never dumps a backlog of the events missed while suppressed.
- **Alert events:** position open / close / flip; **scale-in / scale-out** on a same-coin/same-side size change (issue #10); **withdrawal** on an unexplained equity drop (next bullet); on-demand current-positions view.
- **Withdrawal Alert thresholds: net outflow ≥ 25% of prior equity AND ≥ $1,000** (`WITHDRAWAL_FRACTION_THRESHOLD` / `WITHDRAWAL_FLOOR_USD`, `epigone.withdrawals`; issue #171, ADR-0007 "Out of A4 scope"). Both gates must clear, because either alone misreads half the Universe: a percentage alone pushes a $600 account emptying, a dollar figure alone says nothing about a whale routinely moving $2M between their own venues. The starting proposal from the ticket was kept unchanged after implementation — a quarter of an account leaving inside one 10s poll interval is a decision, never noise, and it sits far above everything the detection deliberately does not model (fees on even a whole-account taker close are basis points; funding over ten seconds is smaller still), so the threshold absorbs that noise rather than the arithmetic having to. Detection is `equity delta minus the PnL those two observations explain`, where the previous observation's unrealized PnL is scaled by the fraction of each position still open — so a full close, a partial close and a flip all attribute their realized part correctly and only real transfers survive. Two further gates guard the same fail-quiet direction and were **added at implementation, beyond the two the ticket named** — both suppress alerts, neither can create one. **Staleness: `MAX_OBSERVATION_GAP_SECONDS = 60`** (six poll intervals): across a longer gap a position could have opened and closed unseen, so the pass records the new equity and says nothing rather than calling an unattributable drop a withdrawal. **Departed-size allowance: `UNSEEN_MOVE_FRACTION = 0.05`** of the notional that left the book this interval: a close prices itself at a moment between two passes, so it can realize materially more loss than the last observed mark showed — the difference is a price move on the departed size, and reads as an unexplained drop, i.e. as a withdrawal. This is the one way a *losing* trade can fire the alert, and it bites hardest on a leveraged position force-closed during a violent move, which is where the alert would be most misleading; charging departed size 5% of its notional covers ordinary moves, so a genuine transfer in the same interval as a close must clear that too. A pass where nothing closed buys no allowance — the common shape of a real transfer, since closing out and withdrawing rarely land in the same ten seconds. Retuning any of the four moves only this alert; they are independent of the min-size floor (#10), which judges a *position's* notional and cannot judge an account-level event.
- **Scale-in/out significance threshold: 25%** (`SCALE_SIGNIFICANCE_THRESHOLD`, `epigone.position_diff` — shared by the REST poll pass and the websocket shadow lane, so retuning it moves both). A same-side notional size change of ≥25% of the last snapshot fires SCALE-IN (bigger) or SCALE-OUT (a partial decrease that isn't a full close); smaller drift stays a silent snapshot update, preserving the pre-#10 partial-close/add behaviour. Conservative by design — over a 10s poll a real coin never moves 25% on price alone, so the signal reflects a deliberate add/trim, not mark-price noise. Change is measured against the last snapshot each poll, so gradual accumulation that never clears 25% in one step stays quiet.
